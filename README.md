## Get started

1. **[Download QuantumWake.exe](https://github.com/peans99/QuantumWake/releases/latest)** and open it. No install, no account, no separate .NET.
2. It finds Star Citizen (LIVE, PTU or EPTU) and reads your log history. A long history takes a few minutes.
3. Right-click the tray icon and open the dashboard, or go to <http://127.0.0.1:31337>.

> **Your game, your data.** Everything is read and kept on your PC. Online
> extras such as current market prices are off until you turn them on.

<details>
<summary>Rather have an installer?</summary>

Download `QuantumWake-<version>-win-x64.msi` from the same page. It installs
into your own profile without asking for an administrator, adds a Start menu
entry, and can be removed from Apps and features. Later versions install over
it.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Windows shows an unknown-publisher warning</summary>

The executable is not code-signed. If you downloaded it from the release page
above, choose <b>More info → Run anyway</b>. Every release is scanned by
Microsoft Defender during the release build; that is a malware scan of the
published files, not a publisher identity check.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Star Citizen is installed somewhere unusual</summary>

Quantum Wake looks on every fixed drive. If it still cannot find the game, run:

```powershell
QuantumWake.exe --path "D:\...\StarCitizen\LIVE"
```
</details>

## Optional extras

| Extra | What it does | Turn it on |
|---|---|---|
| **Overlay** | Short answers on top of the game. Needs **Borderless Windowed**. | Tray icon, or `Ctrl+Alt+O` |
| **Cockpit HUD** | Navigation, tasks, cargo, contracts and the live feed on paired Cougar MFDs. | `Ctrl+Alt+M` · [guide](docs/mfd-mode.md) |
| **Voice commands** | Say "gear down" or "full power to shields" and the app presses the game's key. | Settings → Voice & callouts · [list](docs/voice-commands.md) |
| **Stream Deck** | A 15-key or XL profile built from your own keybindings, in folders. | Settings → Stream Deck · [guide](docs/stream-deck.md) |

| **Live screen reading** | The same readings without taking a screenshot, while the game is in front. | Asked once at start; switch on the **Log** page |

<details>
<summary>Overlay keys</summary>

`Ctrl+Alt+O` shows or hides it. Its pin button makes it click-through;
`Ctrl+Alt+←/→` changes page, `Ctrl+Alt+F` expands it and `Ctrl+Alt+C` shrinks
it to a two- or three-line strip.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What voice and screen reading do with your PC</summary>

**Voice.** Listening is done by Windows' own speech recogniser, on your PC;
nothing is recorded or sent. Commands press the game's key only while Star
Citizen is in front. Say "Quantum Wake copilot on", or hold a push-to-talk key.

**Screen reading.** While Star Citizen is the window in front, a frame is read
about every two seconds, every second while a ship is targeted, and then thrown
away. What is kept is what was read, never the picture: the Fleet Manager, the
map, a loadout, a kiosk, a refinery, the hangar's freight terminal, the
Contracts and Rep apps, your balance and each rock scanned. The Sessions
debrief also gets the rocks you worked, and, only if you turn on **Keep a
record of fights**, the ships you locked. The app never opens the game's
process, reads its memory or presses a key for this. Linux never offers it.
What is kept, and where, is in the [privacy policy](docs/privacy.md).
</details>

## Linux

Quantum Wake runs on Linux for pilots who play Star Citizen under Wine or
Proton. The dashboard, the log and game-data readers, screenshots and copied
locations all work there. The overlay, the cockpit HUD and joystick input stay
on Windows: all three are built on Windows desktop components, and they are
not what a Linux pilot is missing.

<details>
<summary>Install, start, stop and update on Linux</summary>

### Before you start

- **A working Star Citizen install under Wine.** Quantum Wake reads the game's
  own files, so the game has to be installed and started at least once. The
  Linux Users Group is the reference for that: its
  [LUG Helper](https://github.com/starcitizen-lug/lug-helper) installs the
  game, and its wiki's
  [recommended distributions](https://wiki.starcitizen-lug.org/Tips-and-Tricks#recommended-distros)
  are Fedora, Arch, EndeavourOS, openSUSE Tumbleweed, Bazzite, the latest
  Ubuntu release, Debian Testing and Gentoo; it advises against LTS releases
  such as Debian Stable, Ubuntu LTS, Mint, Pop!_OS, Zorin, openSUSE Leap and
  Manjaro. Its preflight check raises `vm.max_map_count` to 16777216 and the
  open-file hard limit to 524288, which the game needs and Quantum Wake does
  not touch. Quantum Wake was built and measured against an install the LUG
  Helper made on Fedora 44.
- **An x86-64 distribution with glibc 2.27 or later**, which is every
  distribution above. The Linux download carries its own copy of .NET, so
  nothing is installed for the dashboard; it needs `libicu` (the dashboard's
  number and date formats come from it) and `libstdc++` (the screenshot
  reader), both present on a desktop install. Microsoft lists Fedora 43 and
  44, Ubuntu 22.04 to 26.04, Debian 13, openSUSE Leap 16 and RHEL 8 to 10 as
  [supported by .NET 10](https://github.com/dotnet/core/blob/main/release-notes/10.0/supported-os.md);
  Arch is not on the list and runs it.
- **For copied `/showlocation` results:** `wl-clipboard` on Wayland (Fedora KDE
  ships it as `wl-paste`), or `xclip` or `xsel` on X11. Without one the
  dashboard says the clipboard cannot be read; screenshots still can.
- **The command-line tool** in the same archive is not self-contained: it needs
  the .NET 10 runtime (`dotnet-runtime-10.0` on Fedora and Ubuntu). The
  dashboard does not.

### Start

Download `QuantumWake-<version>-linux-x64.tar.gz` from the
[release page](https://github.com/peans99/QuantumWake/releases/latest), then:

```bash
tar xzf QuantumWake-*-linux-x64.tar.gz
cd QuantumWake
./quantumwake
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:31337> once the terminal prints `Now listening on` and
`Application started. Press Ctrl+C to shut down`. Nothing opens a browser for
you and there is no tray icon. It finds the game inside the Wine prefix on its
own: a LUG Helper install is read from the helper's settings, and otherwise it
looks in `$WINEPREFIX`, `~/Games`, `~/.wine`, Steam's Proton prefixes, Bottles
and Heroic. If it comes up empty, the dashboard says which prefixes it searched
and asks for the folder, which can be the prefix itself, the `LIVE` folder
under its `drive_c`, or the `C:\...` path copied from the RSI launcher.

```bash
./quantumwake --path ~/Games/star-citizen     # the prefix, or .../StarCitizen/LIVE
./quantumwake --Port 31395                    # another port
./quantumwake --Lan true                      # reachable from another device
```

Its data lives in `~/.local/share/Quantumwake` (or under `$XDG_DATA_HOME`), and
the .NET host unpacks its native libraries to `~/.net/quantumwake/` (42 MB) on
the first start. The first time you switch screenshot reading on it fetches
the two text-recognition models (14 MB) into that data folder.

On the machine this was built on, a Fedora 44 virtual machine, the start took
anywhere from 2 to 90 seconds before the ready line, all of it inside .NET's
own host start-up: an empty ASP.NET application shows the same spread there.
Wait for the ready line rather than reloading the page.

### Stop

`Ctrl+C` in the terminal stops it, within two seconds in every measurement;
closing the terminal stops it too. From anywhere else, `pkill quantumwake`. A
second `./quantumwake` while one is running prints
`Quantum Wake is already running at http://127.0.0.1:31337` and exits.

To have it start with your session and run in the background, a systemd user
service does it:

```bash
mkdir -p ~/.config/systemd/user
cat > ~/.config/systemd/user/quantumwake.service <<'EOF'
[Unit]
Description=Quantum Wake dashboard

[Service]
ExecStart=%h/QuantumWake/quantumwake
Restart=on-failure

[Install]
WantedBy=default.target
EOF
systemctl --user enable --now quantumwake      # start now and at every login
systemctl --user stop quantumwake              # stop; journalctl --user -u quantumwake shows its log
```

Adjust `ExecStart` to wherever you unpacked it.

### Update and remove

The dashboard still checks for new versions and tells you, but it does not
replace itself on Linux; unpack the new archive over the old folder. To remove
Quantum Wake, delete the unpacked folder, `~/.local/share/Quantumwake` and
`~/.net/quantumwake`, and disable the user service if you created one.
</details>


## What Quantum Wake reads

| Source | What it can add |
|---|---|
| **Your logs** | Sessions, travel, ships, contracts, party activity, inventory sightings and confirmed transactions. |
| **Your game install** | Names, items, commodities, recipes, mining deposits, place descriptions and facilities. |
| **Saved screenshots or copied locations** | Optional dated readings when you explicitly ask for them. |
| **The game's screen, live** | Optional, Windows only: the same readings as a screenshot without taking one, plus rocks worked and, if you ask, a record of fights. |
| **Optional community services** | Current prices, shop listings, ship details and wider map/mining coverage. |

The data database lives in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Quantumwake`. Nothing is written
inside Star Citizen unless you explicitly install **item labels** or
**StarStrings**. Both make a backup before replacing the game's English text
file.

## A few useful limits

- It is a logbook, not a live position tracker. Locations come from arrivals,
  inventory and quantum-travel records, and say when they are inferred.
- Current Star Citizen logs do not provide a complete killboard, automatic mining
  history or a complete wallet history. The app labels estimates and partial
  totals instead of filling gaps with zeroes. Reading the screen live fills
  part of the first two, for the time it is on: the ships you locked and the
  rocks you worked, not anything you were not looking at.
- Crew and event totals are floors: players or contracts without the necessary
  log entries cannot be counted.


## Updates, removal and help

The tray icon checks for updates when you ask it to, downloads a new build and
restarts the app. Some updates re-read your history; release notes call that out.

To remove Quantum Wake, delete `QuantumWake.exe`, or uninstall it from Apps and
features if you used the installer, then delete `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Quantumwake`,
which holds your history and is left in place so a reinstall keeps it. Remove item labels from the app first if you
installed them. Linux removal is under [Linux](#linux).

**Settings → Report a problem** makes a small diagnostic file. It contains
parser counts, game builds and integration state, but not logs, account
identifiers, handles, folder names or API keys. You can inspect it before
attaching it to an issue.

## Release notes

Releases before 1.0 are in [docs/release-history.md](docs/release-history.md).

### 1.2.38

- **The Stream Deck page opens properly again from a refresh or a link.**
  Since 1.2.36, opening the dashboard straight onto the Stream Deck page left
  it blank, with no categories and no key preview, until you went to another
  page and came back.

### 1.2.37

**The Command deck starts with your work.** It opens on **Continue your work**: your tracked flight plan with its next stop, the shopping list still being filled, an open checklist, refinery orders waiting or still refining, and the event you are playing, each a card that takes you to it. Only what is actually under way gets a card. The workspaces underneath now carry the names the Operations menu uses (Shopping lists, Routes, Contracts and so on), with what each is for beneath. **Mining has moved from Reference to Operations**: its refinery orders, radar and fracture charge are work in progress rather than something you look up.

**Saved setups are kept like kits.** Routes presets lived in the browser, so the overlay never saw one made in a browser and no backup carried them; Stream Deck choices were not kept at all and reset on every reload. Both are now kept by the app, in their own file and in your backup, the way kits and Garage builds always were. Your existing presets move across by themselves the first time the Routes page opens. The Stream Deck page comes back to the model, bindings and commands you last had, and can keep a choice under a name. Backups now use format 3: this version restores older backups, and an older version asks you to update before restoring a new one rather than dropping your setups.

**A plan remembers the ship it was planned for.** Trade runs, circuits and split loads now keep the ship the Routes planner was sizing for and the hold it assumed, with where that figure came from; hauling runs keep the ship the log had you in. The flight plan and the Itinerary show it, and when the log says you are flying something else, the plan says so - loads sized for a Hercules do not go in a Cutlass.

**A suit you design becomes a kit.** The Armoury's suit calculator has **Save as kit**: the pieces worn, the guns holstered and everything carried are kept as a kit on the Loadout page, repeats counted. **Prepare it** then opens that kit's Prepare panel, which says what you already own and where the rest is sold, and from there it becomes a shopping list. Before, a kit could only be saved from what the log last saw you wearing.

**Every planner writes a run sheet you can edit.** A trade circuit and a shopping-list run used to land in the flight plan as notes, so their amounts and prices could not be changed once flown and the profit could not be worked out again. They now write the same priced buy and sell lines a single trade run always did: the circuit's outbound buy and sale, the return load bought where the outbound one is sold, and its sale; the shopping run's one buy per item at the counter chosen for it, with the amount and that counter's price.

**Hauling runs, shared with a group.** Under the hauling run on Jobs there is now **Share the run**: keep a list of the wingmates you haul with and the ship each one brings, tick who is flying, say where each one is, and your open contracts are shared out so the whole run is done soonest, with the longest single ship’s run made as short as it can be. Each ship gets its stops in flying order, the distance of each hop and what is aboard after each stop. Pickups always come before drops, no ship is ever given more than its hold, and a contract stays on one ship unless you tick *split* for a run of party-shared contracts. Your share can be made your flight plan; a wingmate’s copies as lines for party chat. With only you ticked, it orders your own run by distance. Distances are straight lines between planets and moons from the community star map, not the route flown; two stops on one body count as none, and a place with no position on file makes the figure a floor. **Put in order** on the Itinerary page now also takes the nearest stop next when it knows the distance.

**The locked ship as a real 3D model.** The MFD Target page now shows the hull you have locked as the game’s own 3D model, the same one the 3D view draws, turning slowly, with a red glow while it is hostile. The flat picture shows first and stays if your install has no model for that hull. It is kept light because the game is running beside it: no effects or shadows, 24 frames a second, and it is released the moment the lock or the page goes. If you set the Garage to silhouettes, for every ship or for that one, the Target page keeps the silhouette too.

**Callouts no longer hide the Target page.** On Target, Callouts and Charge, a spoken line now appears in a slim strip where the page title was, so the locked ship, its hull and its range stay in sight through a busy fight. Other pages still get the large card. A recorded fight with a line every four or five seconds showed why: the large card stayed up from the lock to the kill, and the Target page underneath was never seen. Also fixed: one lock now reads "1 lock this session", not "1 locks".

**English only.** The French translation is gone. Few people read the app in French, and keeping it complete meant translating every new sentence before a change could ship. If you had French chosen, the app opens in English after this update and there is nothing to change; the language picker in Settings → General is gone with it. Names from the game were always English and are unchanged.

**What the voice says is on the MFD too.** Every line the voice says (a callout, a mission toast, a copilot reply) now comes up over the page on the frame that follows the action, in large type, for six seconds. ### 1.2.15

Two new ways to press the game's keys, both built from your own keybindings: voice commands, and Star Citizen profiles for Stream Deck. The overlay gets a much smaller compact view and a quieter look over the game, and the Cockpit HUD's Target page draws the ship the way the Garage does.

#### Voice commands

**Voice commands: say it instead of finding the key.** For the things you do once a session and would rather not give a button to. Settings → Voice & callouts has a new Voice commands switch, off until you turn it on. Say "Quantum Wake copilot on", or hold a push-to-talk button or key you pick, and the app listens for a short list of commands:

- Gun, mining, salvage, scan, quantum, combat and nav mode.
- Ping and scan target.
- Open and close all doors.
- Power to shields.
- Request landing or departure.
- The three mining modules, switch laser, toggle beam, and laser power.
- Self destruct and jettison cargo, which both wait for you to say "confirm". With push-to-talk, "confirm" has to be said with the button held too, so a "confirm" from someone else on comms cancels instead.

Each command presses the key the game has for that action, either yours from your keybindings or the game's default, held for as long as the game's own rules ask, and only while Star Citizen is the window in front. The list shows which key each command will press. It says plainly when an action is only on your stick or has no key, because voice can press keyboard keys and mouse buttons but not stick buttons. Every answer the copilot gives, such as "Copilot on", "Mining mode", "Confirm self destruct?" or "Game not in front", is said in the same recorded voice as the callouts, and each command has a Hear it button. Listening is done on your PC by the speech recogniser Windows already carries, told only these phrases. Nothing is recorded or sent. Windows only. It has not been tried in the game itself yet, so try one command first to check the game takes the key.

**It understands the way you actually say things.** Each command listens for patterns rather than one exact sentence, so all of these work: "full power to shields", "max shields", "shields to the right", "full shield back", "boost the rear shields", "gear down", "switch to mining", "request takeoff". Any command can start with "copilot" and end with "please". It also covers:
- The six shield faces: front, rear, left, right, top and bottom. Left also answers to port and right to starboard.
- Balance shields, more and less shields, minimum shields, and shields on, off and toggle.
- Weapons and engines power, and balance power.
- Gear up, gear down and toggle landing gear.

Settings → Voice & callouts → Voice commands shows a few example wordings for each command. Recognition was checked with the Windows voice speaking into a file: the long wordings come back at 99% confidence. "Nav" is a word the Windows recogniser cannot hear at all, so say "navigation mode".

**Free keys for commands that have none.** Voice can only press keyboard keys, and on many installs several of these actions are on a stick or on nothing. The Voice commands list shows them and offers a free key for each, such as Right Ctrl + Numpad 1 or Right Ctrl + F1. These are chords the game leaves unused, and the app skips any key already used by your profile or by any of the game's defaults. One click writes them the same way the Controls page writes bindings: into your profile, after a backup, when the game is closed, or as an import file while it runs. Your stick bindings stay as they are.

**Safer around self destruct and shields.**
- "Abort self destruct" presses the self destruct key again, which starts a countdown when none is running. So it presses at once only within five minutes of a self destruct the copilot itself confirmed. Otherwise it asks "Confirm abort self destruct?" first.
- "Shields off" waits for "confirm", because "on" and "off" sound alike.
- A chord the game writes modifier last, such as "F7 + LAlt" for less power to shields, is pressed modifier first. Pressed in the written order, F7 alone would raise the shields instead of lowering them.

**Star Citizen profiles for Stream Deck.** Settings has a Stream Deck tab, beside Controls, for choosing commands and generating a 15-key or XL profile from the selected game installation’s keyboard and mouse bindings, in folders by category. The voice is its own switch on the Log page, off until you turn it on, with a Hear it button to try it, and uses the speech voice Windows already has; if a callout cannot be played - no voice installed, no audio device - the Log page says why. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing about a fight is stored. It was built and measured against a 2 h 24 min recording of a Tactical Strike Group run rather than a live game, so the first fights with it on are its real test: hostile is read from the colour of the target's label and from factions seen shooting (Vanduul, Outlaws, Shattered Blade), and a label off to the side of the screen can go unread.

**Show it without a fight.** Started with `--Replay=<folder>` (or `QUANTUMWAKE_REPLAY` set to the folder), the app reads a folder of recorded frames, one a second, in place of the game window - pull them from an OBS recording with ffmpeg at one frame a second - and everything after that is what a real fight runs: the Target card, the MFD pages and the voice. Live reading still has to be switched on; nothing about the replay is stored.

**The MFD draws the ship you have targeted.** The Target page leads with a picture of the lock: the game's own silhouette of the hull - from its vehicle icons, which cover about four hulls in five - glowing red while it is hostile and dimmed while it is only the likeliest guess, or, for a hull with no icon (the Paladin, the Perseus and the Scythe among them) and for a target nothing can name yet, the game's own target hologram cut live from your screen, damage colours and all.

**A Target card, and Combat on the MFD.** While live reading sees a lock, a Target card heads the Now page: the ship's name, or the hulls its signature fits; hostile or not, its faction, speed, range and cross-section; and the last few callouts. It turns red in a fight, and the overlay's Combat layout carries it. The MFD gains a fifth menu, Combat, with a Target page and a Callouts page, both refreshed every second and a half while they are on screen. Combat takes Home's fifth button, which used to jump straight to Navigation; Navigation is now the first page under Flight.

**Read the game's screen live, if you want to (prototype).** Quantum Wake can now read Star Citizen's screen while you play, starting with the mining scan panel: select a rock and its scan lands on the Mining page and in the Log without taking a screenshot, one reading per rock. It is off until you say yes: the app asks once when it starts, and the switch is on the Log page under Read from the game. It only reads while Star Citizen is the window in front, copies the same pixels a screenshot would, reads them on your PC and throws each frame away; it never touches the game's process, memory or input. It needs borderless or windowed mode, not exclusive fullscreen, and the Windows app rather than the server on its own. CIG's terms neither allow nor forbid reading the screen in so many words - the question says so - and the privacy policy now covers it. While it reads, it keeps a small log of when each frame was taken and how long it took to read - no screen text - in the app's data folder, so a play session can be measured afterwards.

**Ship parts say what they do, in game.** With item labels installed, the description of a shield, power plant, cooler, quantum drive, ship gun or missile now lists the figures the game leaves out: shield HP, regeneration and its delays; power and cooling output; quantum speed, spool and cooldown; a gun's damage per shot, fire rate, damage a second, projectile speed and range; a missile's damage, speed, lock time and lock range. They are read from your own install and sit with the game's own Size and Grade lines, worded the same way - 477 descriptions on the current patch. Where the game files leave a figure open it is left out rather than guessed: guns whose fire rate depends on how they cycle show no rate, and charged and distortion guns show no per-shot damage. Turn it off under Settings, Item labels, then install the labels again and restart the game.

**Missions no longer runs into its neighbour.** A contract with a long internal name used to print it on one line straight across the Offered by column; it now wraps inside its own cell.

**The links in the Shopping lists header are readable.** Market, Parts and Mining showed in the browser's default dark blue on the Operations command deck; they now match every other link in the app.

### 1.0.2

**A privacy policy.** Settings → About now links to a page that says what the app reads on your computer, what it keeps and where, and every occasion it reaches the internet, with who it contacts, what is sent and the switch that controls it. It also says plainly that UEX credentials, if you use price reporting, are kept as plain text in your user profile. The About text no longer claims that nothing is written to the game folder: installing the item labels does write there, and it now says so.

**An installer, if you want one.** Each release now also carries `QuantumWake-<version>-win-x64.msi`: the same app, installed into your own profile without asking for an administrator, with a Start menu entry and an entry in Apps and features to remove it. A later version installs over an earlier one, and an older one refuses to install over a newer. Uninstalling leaves your history in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Quantumwake`, so a reinstall picks up where you were. It also installs silently (`msiexec /i … /quiet`), which is what the Microsoft Store needs. The plain `QuantumWake.exe` is still there and still the whole app on its own.

**A Microsoft Store edition, on its way.** Quantum Wake is being packaged for the Microsoft Store. A copy installed from the Store keeps its history in the same folder as the download, so moving to it loses nothing, and when a new version is out its notice sends you to the Store, which installs it, rather than replacing the app itself.
