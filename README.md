# Quantum Wake

**A private Star Citizen logbook for Windows.**

Quantum Wake reads the logs Star Citizen already writes and turns them into a
personal record of your flights: where you went, what you flew, contracts,
trades, ships, gear and the things you choose to plan next. It runs on your PC;
there is no account and no required online service.

[![CI](https://github.com/peans99/QuantumWake/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/peans99/QuantumWake/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

> **Your game, your data.** Quantum Wake reads your logs and game files locally.
> Network features such as current market prices are off until you turn them on.

The 3D view reads your own Star Citizen install, `Data.p4k`, so it shows the
models of the patch you are actually playing. Nothing is downloaded for it.

The dashboard is for planning and history. The optional overlay gives the same
short answers over the game, and the optional Cougar MFD mode keeps navigation,
tasks and cargo close to the cockpit. The dashboard, overlay and MFD pages are
in English or French; choose under **Settings → General**.

## Start here

### 1. Download and run

**[Download QuantumWake.exe](https://github.com/peans99/QuantumWake/releases/latest)**,
move it somewhere you keep applications, then open it. There is no account or
separate .NET download.

If you would rather have it installed, download
`QuantumWake-<version>-win-x64.msi` from the same page instead. It installs into
your own profile without asking for an administrator, adds a Start menu entry,
and can be removed from Apps and features. Later versions install over it.

Quantum Wake starts in the notification area. Right-click its tray icon to open
the dashboard or overlay. The dashboard is also available at
<http://127.0.0.1:31337> while the app is running.

On Linux there is a separate download and no tray icon; see [Linux](#linux)
below.

<details>
<summary>Windows shows an unknown-publisher warning</summary>

The executable is not code-signed. If you downloaded it from the release page
above, choose <b>More info → Run anyway</b>. Every release is scanned by
Microsoft Defender during the release build; that is a malware scan of the
published files, not a publisher identity check.
</details>

### 2. Let it read your history

Quantum Wake finds LIVE, PTU and EPTU installations on fixed drives. On first
start it reads `Game.log` and its backups, then watches only the current log as
you play. A large log history can take a few minutes; the app shows the reading
progress.

If your installation is in an unusual location, run:

```powershell
QuantumWake.exe --path "D:\...\StarCitizen\LIVE"
```

### 3. Pick the view you need

| When you want to… | Open… |
|---|---|
| Check your last location, ship or wake-up point | **Now** |
| Find a station, city, outpost or nearby service | **Map** |
| Lay out a flight plan, or merge a hauling run into one | **Itinerary** |
| Read the live feed, or a screenshot you just took | **Log** |
| Plan the work: the Command deck, a cargo or rep run, contracts, reputation, checklists, blueprints, Wikelo trades or an event | **Operations** |
| Review a flight, session, place, server, crewmate or death | **Flight** |
| Follow money in and out, your cargo, or commodity prices | **Economy** |
| Look up ships, components, items, missions, encounters, loot odds, mining or crafting, or open any hull in 3D | **Reference** |
| See your ships, fittings, loadout and stash, bench a part in the Garage, or compare guns, add up a suit and check it against a climate in the Armoury | **Hangar** |
| Keep a small answer visible in Star Citizen | **Overlay** |

## Optional extras

### Overlay

The overlay starts off. Star Citizen needs **Borderless Windowed** mode for it
to sit above the game. Use the tray icon or `Ctrl+Alt+O` to show it. Its pin
button makes it click-through; `Ctrl+Alt+←/→` changes page, `Ctrl+Alt+F`
expands it and `Ctrl+Alt+C` shrinks it to a two- or three-line strip.

### Cockpit HUD

If you use paired Cougar MFDs, Quantum Wake can show navigation, tasks, cargo,
contracts, money and the live feed on them. Read the [Cockpit HUD guide](docs/mfd-mode.md)
when you are ready to set it up; `Ctrl+Alt+M` opens MFD setup from anywhere.

### Voice commands

Turn on Voice commands in Settings → Voice & callouts, then say "Quantum Wake
copilot on" or hold a push-to-talk key, and say things like "gear down",
"mining mode" or "full power to shields". The app presses the game's key for
that action, only while Star Citizen is in front. Listening is done by Windows'
own speech recogniser, on your PC; nothing is recorded or sent. See
[voice-commands.md](docs/voice-commands.md) for the full list.

### Stream Deck

Settings → Stream Deck builds a Star Citizen profile for a 15-key or XL Stream
Deck from your own keybindings, with commands in folders by category, and
installs the small plugin that presses them. Rebuild it after changing your
bindings in game. Windows only; see [stream-deck.md](docs/stream-deck.md).

### Screenshots and copied locations

You can ask Quantum Wake to read a saved Star Citizen screenshot or a copied
`/showlocation` result. This can add facts the log does not carry, such as a
kiosk balance, fitted ship parts, your rank with an organisation in the Rep
app, or the pickups and SCU on a hauling contract in the Contracts app. It reads
the saved file locally; it never captures your screen or reads game memory.

## Linux

Quantum Wake runs on Linux for pilots who play Star Citizen under Wine or
Proton. The dashboard, the log and game-data readers, screenshots and copied
locations all work there. The overlay, the cockpit HUD and joystick input stay
on Windows: all three are built on Windows desktop components, and they are
not what a Linux pilot is missing.

### Before you start

- **A working Star Citizen install under Wine.** Quantum Wake reads the game's
  own files, so the game has to be installed and started at least once. The
  Linux Users Group is the reference for that: its
  [LUG Helper](https://github.com/starcitizen-lug/lug-helper) installs the
  game, and its wiki's
  [recommended distributions](https://wiki.starcitizen-lug.org/Tips-and-Tricks#recommended-distros)
  are Fedora, Arch, EndeavourOS, openSUSE Tumbleweed, Bazzite, the latest
  Ubuntu release, Debian Testing and Gentoo; it advises against LTS releases
  such as Debian Stable, Ubuntu LTS, Mint, Pop!_OS, Zorin, openSUSE Leap and
  Manjaro. Its preflight check raises `vm.max_map_count` to 16777216 and the
  open-file hard limit to 524288, which the game needs and Quantum Wake does
  not touch. Quantum Wake was built and measured against an install the LUG
  Helper made on Fedora 44.
- **An x86-64 distribution with glibc 2.27 or later**, which is every
  distribution above. The Linux download carries its own copy of .NET, so
  nothing is installed for the dashboard; it needs `libicu` (the dashboard's
  French and date formats come from it) and `libstdc++` (the screenshot
  reader), both present on a desktop install. Microsoft lists Fedora 43 and
  44, Ubuntu 22.04 to 26.04, Debian 13, openSUSE Leap 16 and RHEL 8 to 10 as
  [supported by .NET 10](https://github.com/dotnet/core/blob/main/release-notes/10.0/supported-os.md);
  Arch is not on the list and runs it.
- **For copied `/showlocation` results:** `wl-clipboard` on Wayland (Fedora KDE
  ships it as `wl-paste`), or `xclip` or `xsel` on X11. Without one the
  dashboard says the clipboard cannot be read; screenshots still can.
- **The command-line tool** in the same archive is not self-contained: it needs
  the .NET 10 runtime (`dotnet-runtime-10.0` on Fedora and Ubuntu). The
  dashboard does not.

### Start

Download `QuantumWake-<version>-linux-x64.tar.gz` from the
[release page](https://github.com/peans99/QuantumWake/releases/latest), then:

```bash
tar xzf QuantumWake-*-linux-x64.tar.gz
cd QuantumWake
./quantumwake
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:31337> once the terminal prints `Now listening on` and
`Application started. Press Ctrl+C to shut down`. Nothing opens a browser for
you and there is no tray icon. It finds the game inside the Wine prefix on its
own: a LUG Helper install is read from the helper's settings, and otherwise it
looks in `$WINEPREFIX`, `~/Games`, `~/.wine`, Steam's Proton prefixes, Bottles
and Heroic. If it comes up empty, the dashboard says which prefixes it searched
and asks for the folder, which can be the prefix itself, the `LIVE` folder
under its `drive_c`, or the `C:\...` path copied from the RSI launcher.

```bash
./quantumwake --path ~/Games/star-citizen     # the prefix, or .../StarCitizen/LIVE
./quantumwake --Port 31395                    # another port
./quantumwake --Lan true                      # reachable from another device
```

Its data lives in `~/.local/share/Quantumwake` (or under `$XDG_DATA_HOME`), and
the .NET host unpacks its native libraries to `~/.net/quantumwake/` (42 MB) on
the first start. The first time you switch screenshot reading on it fetches
the two text-recognition models (14 MB) into that data folder.

On the machine this was built on, a Fedora 44 virtual machine, the start took
anywhere from 2 to 90 seconds before the ready line, all of it inside .NET's
own host start-up: an empty ASP.NET application shows the same spread there.
Wait for the ready line rather than reloading the page.

### Stop

`Ctrl+C` in the terminal stops it, within two seconds in every measurement;
closing the terminal stops it too. From anywhere else, `pkill quantumwake`. A
second `./quantumwake` while one is running prints
`Quantum Wake is already running at http://127.0.0.1:31337` and exits.

To have it start with your session and run in the background, a systemd user
service does it:

```bash
mkdir -p ~/.config/systemd/user
cat > ~/.config/systemd/user/quantumwake.service <<'EOF'
[Unit]
Description=Quantum Wake dashboard

[Service]
ExecStart=%h/QuantumWake/quantumwake
Restart=on-failure

[Install]
WantedBy=default.target
EOF
systemctl --user enable --now quantumwake      # start now and at every login
systemctl --user stop quantumwake              # stop; journalctl --user -u quantumwake shows its log
```

Adjust `ExecStart` to wherever you unpacked it.

### Update and remove

The dashboard still checks for new versions and tells you, but it does not
replace itself on Linux; unpack the new archive over the old folder. To remove
Quantum Wake, delete the unpacked folder, `~/.local/share/Quantumwake` and
`~/.net/quantumwake`, and disable the user service if you created one.


## What Quantum Wake reads

| Source | What it can add |
|---|---|
| **Your logs** | Sessions, travel, ships, contracts, party activity, inventory sightings and confirmed transactions. |
| **Your game install** | Names, items, commodities, recipes, mining deposits, place descriptions and facilities. |
| **Saved screenshots or copied locations** | Optional dated readings when you explicitly ask for them. |
| **Optional community services** | Current prices, shop listings, ship details and wider map/mining coverage. |

The data database lives in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Quantumwake`. Nothing is written
inside Star Citizen unless you explicitly install **item labels** or
**StarStrings**. Both make a backup before replacing the game's English text
file.

## A few useful limits

- It is a logbook, not a live position tracker. Locations come from arrivals,
  inventory and quantum-travel records, and say when they are inferred.
- Current Star Citizen logs do not provide a complete killboard, automatic mining
  history or a complete wallet history. The app labels estimates and partial
  totals instead of filling gaps with zeroes.
- Crew and event totals are floors: players or contracts without the necessary
  log entries cannot be counted.


## Updates, removal and help

The tray icon checks for updates when you ask it to, downloads a new build and
restarts the app. Some updates re-read your history; release notes call that out.

To remove Quantum Wake, delete `QuantumWake.exe`, or uninstall it from Apps and
features if you used the installer, then delete `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Quantumwake`,
which holds your history and is left in place so a reinstall keeps it. Remove item labels from the app first if you
installed them. Linux removal is under [Linux](#linux).

**Settings → Report a problem** makes a small diagnostic file. It contains
parser counts, game builds and integration state, but not logs, account
identifiers, handles, folder names or API keys. You can inspect it before
attaching it to an issue.

### 1.2.15

Two new ways to press the game's keys, both built from your own keybindings: voice commands, and Star Citizen profiles for Stream Deck. The overlay gets a much smaller compact view and a quieter look over the game, and the Cockpit HUD's Target page draws the ship the way the Garage does.

#### Voice commands

**Voice commands: say it instead of finding the key.** For the things you do once a session and would rather not give a button to. Settings → Voice & callouts has a new Voice commands switch, off until you turn it on. Say "Quantum Wake copilot on", or hold a push-to-talk button or key you pick, and the app listens for a short list of commands:

- Gun, mining, salvage, scan, quantum, combat and nav mode.
- Ping and scan target.
- Open and close all doors.
- Power to shields.
- Request landing or departure.
- The three mining modules, switch laser, toggle beam, and laser power.
- Self destruct and jettison cargo, which both wait for you to say "confirm". With push-to-talk, "confirm" has to be said with the button held too, so a "confirm" from someone else on comms cancels instead.

Each command presses the key the game has for that action, either yours from your keybindings or the game's default, held for as long as the game's own rules ask, and only while Star Citizen is the window in front. The list shows which key each command will press. It says plainly when an action is only on your stick or has no key, because voice can press keyboard keys and mouse buttons but not stick buttons. Every answer the copilot gives, such as "Copilot on", "Mining mode", "Confirm self destruct?" or "Game not in front", is said in the same recorded voice as the callouts, and each command has a Hear it button. Listening is done on your PC by the speech recogniser Windows already carries, told only these phrases. Nothing is recorded or sent. Windows only. It has not been tried in the game itself yet, so try one command first to check the game takes the key.

**It understands the way you actually say things.** Each command listens for patterns rather than one exact sentence, so all of these work: "full power to shields", "max shields", "shields to the right", "full shield back", "boost the rear shields", "gear down", "switch to mining", "request takeoff". Any command can start with "copilot" and end with "please". It also covers:
- The six shield faces: front, rear, left, right, top and bottom. Left also answers to port and right to starboard.
- Balance shields, more and less shields, minimum shields, and shields on, off and toggle.
- Weapons and engines power, and balance power.
- Gear up, gear down and toggle landing gear.

Settings → Voice & callouts → Voice commands shows a few example wordings for each command. Recognition was checked with the Windows voice speaking into a file: the long wordings come back at 99% confidence. "Nav" is a word the Windows recogniser cannot hear at all, so say "navigation mode".

**Free keys for commands that have none.** Voice can only press keyboard keys, and on many installs several of these actions are on a stick or on nothing. The Voice commands list shows them and offers a free key for each, such as Right Ctrl + Numpad 1 or Right Ctrl + F1. These are chords the game leaves unused, and the app skips any key already used by your profile or by any of the game's defaults. One click writes them the same way the Controls page writes bindings: into your profile, after a backup, when the game is closed, or as an import file while it runs. Your stick bindings stay as they are.

**Safer around self destruct and shields.**
- "Abort self destruct" presses the self destruct key again, which starts a countdown when none is running. So it presses at once only within five minutes of a self destruct the copilot itself confirmed. Otherwise it asks "Confirm abort self destruct?" first.
- "Shields off" waits for "confirm", because "on" and "off" sound alike.
- A chord the game writes modifier last, such as "F7 + LAlt" for less power to shields, is pressed modifier first. Pressed in the written order, F7 alone would raise the shields instead of lowering them.

#### Stream Deck

**Star Citizen profiles for Stream Deck.** Settings has a Stream Deck tab, beside Controls, for choosing commands and generating a 15-key or XL profile from the selected game installation’s keyboard and mouse bindings, in folders by category. Seven ready-made categories (Flight, Combat, Power, Mining, Salvage, Ground Vehicle, and Ship & Utilities, 88 commands) are selected by default, with an optional Extended set, and any other game command can be added. Open in Stream Deck hands the profile to the Stream Deck app’s import prompt, installing the companion plugin the first time. The page explains the actions it cannot send, such as ones bound only to a stick. Buttons work like the keys they stand for: a command the game only fires after a hold, such as self destruct after half a second, still needs that hold on the Stream Deck, and the page says how long next to each one, and which ones do one thing on a tap and another when held. Rebuild the profile after changing your bindings in game. Windows only, Stream Deck 7.1 or later. It has been checked against Stream Deck's profile format, not yet pressed on a deck in the game, so start with Landing Gear to check your setup.

**Buttons you can read at a glance.** Commands have function icons, bright labels on dark key faces, and spaced keyboard hints. Long names wrap between words, and the game's untranslated command names (like character_customizer_yaw_left) break at their underscores. Categories have their own colours and symbols; folder keys show only their own name under the folder mark, and Back and More have arrows of their own. The builder shows the plugin's own button artwork, with the full label and binding when a key is selected. XL previews keep the keys readable on narrow screens by scrolling.

#### Overlay

**A smaller overlay.** The overlay's compact view (the ▾ button or Ctrl+Alt+C) is now a thin strip instead of a 300-pixel card. It shows where you are, or where your jump is going, and your ship. Under that is the next thing to do, such as your plan's next stop or a contract, but only when there is one. A third line appears only while something needs your attention: a target lock (red when hostile), a fracture under way, or an event you track. When none of those is happening it shows your cash, if the app has read it. A line with nothing to say is left out, and the window shrinks or grows to fit, so the strip is usually two lines tall and at most 380 pixels wide. Click it to get the full overlay back. The full overlay is shorter too:
- The "GAME LOG" tag on every field is gone.
- Ship, pilot and deaths are left out when there is nothing to show, rather than drawn as dashes.
- The big focus banner is one line, and is hidden when it would only repeat where you are.
- "What to do next" keeps its place, plan and next stops, without the planning controls, services, insurance or stash.
- The feed shows the last five events instead of twelve.

Fullscreen (Ctrl+Alt+F) and the dashboard still show everything.

### 1.0.2

**A privacy policy.** Settings → About now links to a page that says what the app reads on your computer, what it keeps and where, and every occasion it reaches the internet, with who it contacts, what is sent and the switch that controls it. **A Microsoft Store edition, on its way.** Quantum Wake is being packaged for the Microsoft Store.
