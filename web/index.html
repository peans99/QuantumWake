            Crew

            The people you fly with. Star Citizen 4.9 and 4.10 log no party roster — there is no join event carrying a member list and no player id anywhere — so this is built from the only lines that name anyone: the toasts announcing that somebody in your party came online or dropped. Every number here is a floor, never a total. A friend who was already online when you grouped up and stayed until you logged off produces no toast at all, so an evening flown together can leave no trace. What these rows can be trusted to say is that these people were there — never that nobody else was.

                Item labels

                Star Citizen reads its English text from one file at startup, so replacing that file changes what the game calls things. It is text only — no binary, no memory, nothing the anti-cheat looks at — and it is the only thing this app writes outside its own folder. Restart the game for a change to take effect.

                StarStrings

                A community text mod by MrKraken that rewrites the game’s English text to read more usefully: contracts that award blueprints are tagged [BP], item names are shortened, contracts show the reputation they pay, and the mining guide is sorted by rarity. github.com/MrKraken/StarStrings — not written by, or affiliated with, this app.

                It works alongside the labels below. Both replace the same file, so whichever is installed second would otherwise remove the first. Install these in either order and the labels are rebuilt on top of StarStrings' text, so contract reputation tags and item labels are both there. Removing either one puts back exactly what it displaced.

                This writes into your game folder. It puts two files in your LIVE directory — Data\Localization\english\global.ini and USER.cfg — and nothing else; anything else in the download is refused. Whatever is already there is copied aside first, and Remove puts it back. A game patch may replace it, and then it simply is not installed any more.

                Label your items

                Writes what this app knows into the names themselves, in one bracket at the end: [S2B] is a size 2 grade B component, [H] is heavy armour, and * means nothing is known to sell it — so a thing worth carrying is obvious while you are standing over it. Four characters, because the median item name is already 21 and a third are over 24.

                It is a floor, not a rarity rating. Two things say an item is sold: your own receipts, which cannot be wrong because the game charged you, and UEX, which is broad but crowd-sourced — it misses 29 of the 106 items these logs prove were bought at a kiosk. Nothing lists what shops stock, so an unmarked item means nobody has told us otherwise, never “confirmed common”. Ship components and internals are left out: marking every name would mark most of them, and a mark on almost everything says nothing.

When there is a newer one you are offered it, with its size, and nothing is fetched until you say so. The same check sits on the toolbar, under Settings.

                Last wipe

                A data wipe resets what your account holds, so logs from before one describe an account you no longer have — adding those sales to this account's income makes a wrong number, not a bigger one. Sessions before this date are kept and still parsed, they are simply not counted. Moving the date back brings every one of them straight back.

                Wipes come at different depths — some take aUEC and leave the hangar, some clear inventories and leave balances. Tick only what this one actually took; everything left unticked keeps its whole history.

                About

                The goal is a frictionless companion for Star Citizen — one that gathers the useful information the game already writes down, and hands it back to fellow players without asking anything of them.

                It exists because I went looking for it and it was not there. There are good Game.log tools, but none of them was a whole product built around an org and the things I wanted to keep track of — so I built the one I had been looking for, and it is free for the community to use, and meant to stay that way.

                No account, no configuration, no upload. It reads Game.log and the backups beside it, resolves the names from your own copy of the game, and shows you what is there. Nothing is written to the game directory, and the app connects to the internet only where you have said it may — the optional datasets from the Settings page, and, if you turn them on, the version check and the market-price refresh. Those last two are the only ones that go out without a click, and both are off until you say otherwise.

                There is a Discord for questions, bug reports and whatever the logs turn up next: discord.gg/AV3cDzRs39. It is the place to say a page looks wrong, which is worth more than it sounds: most of what this reads was found by somebody noticing a number that could not be right.

                Help & FAQ

                Short answers for the parts of the game, logs and app that are easy to misread.

                Just installed?Start the app, wait for the first log read, then open the dashboard from the tray. It reads every backup the game kept — a few seconds for a hundred and fifty files.
                Something looks wrong?Check the source beside the number first; a missing log signal is not the same as zero, and the app says which it is.
                Need to report it?Settings → Report a problem makes a small diagnostic file you can read before sharing.
                Data sources

                These labels name the evidence behind an answer, not a promise that it is current. A reading from a screenshot is dated; community data is optional and not live; an inference says so beside the source.

                Getting started

                What is the orange banner about a patch and a wipe?

                Every account starts over when a patch wipes, so the app counts your history from a line. When a new game version appears in the logs it asks whether that patch wiped: It wiped — start there moves the line to that patch; It did not wipe leaves it where it is. Nothing is deleted either way — Settings → Last wipe moves the line again, and sessions before it stay in the list, just uncounted.

                Can I see it on a tablet or a second screen?

                Yes. Start the app with start.ps1 -Lan and it listens on the network rather than only on this machine. From another device the dashboard is read-only: everything that changes something — settings, the wipe line, installing labels — is refused off-machine, because there is no login and there should not need to be one.

                Which keys do anything?

                In the dashboard the left and right arrows cycle through the views. For the overlay, Ctrl+Alt+O shows or hides it and Ctrl+Alt+F switches between widget size and full screen; both work while the game has focus. The Cougar MFD frames answer their own physical buttons once MFD mode is set up.

                Where does it keep my data?

                Under %LOCALAPPDATA%\Quantumwake: the session database, the things you typed, screen readings, points and any files you were sent. Nothing is written into the game folder unless you install item labels or StarStrings, and both keep a backup of the file they replace in the app's own folder.

                Reading the numbers

                Why is my pledged ship, armour or gun missing?

                Nothing that arrives in your account is written to Game.log. A pledge, pack, rental or website purchase appears here only after you fly the ship, wear the armour or move the item: that is the first moment the game writes a line to read.

                Why are my totals lower than I expected?

                Sessions from before the last wipe are kept but not counted, because a wipe ends one account and starts another. Settings → Last wipe says how many sessions that is and lets you move the date or choose what the wipe took. Nothing is deleted; move the date back and it returns.

                Why is the trading rate not my income?

                Commodity sales are the only income the logs record. Contract payouts, bounties and mission rewards are never written, so the rate on the Now page is what trading makes an hour and is labelled a floor: for a hauler it is close to the whole story, for a bounty hunter it is a fraction of it. Time spent in menus is left out of the hours.

                What does the ~ on the Ledger mean?

                Item purchases are confirmed by the game server and appear as they were. A cargo trade is logged as the amount you asked the kiosk for, and the kiosk's answer is never written — so those rows carry a ~. The place on a row is inferred from your last arrival, not logged with the sale, and the Ledger says so.

                Where did the kill counter go?

                Star Citizen 4.9 and 4.10 do not write kill or vehicle-destruction lines to Game.log. A counter fixed at zero would look broken, so it is absent. The parser for the older format remains ready if the game starts writing those lines again.

                Why does the map not show where I am exactly?

                No player position is logged. Location comes from discrete signals such as inventory requests, quantum routes and spawns, so the map is a topology rather than a radar. Every estimate carries its confidence instead of pretending to precision it does not have. For an exact position, see the points questions below — the game will hand you one on request.

                How does it know where I will wake up?

                It does not, exactly. The game never logs where your regeneration point is set, so the Wake up at card infers it from the last medical bed or insurance office you used and says which. A bed you were carried to once is not necessarily your regen, and the card leads with that.

                Points, screenshots and the cockpit

                How do I mark a point of interest?

                Type /showlocation in the game's chat: the game copies your exact coordinates to the clipboard, which is the only place it ever gives them away. Then, on the Log tab, press Read copied location — or tick Watch clipboard and the app notices on its own — and pin the reading. It lands on Flight → Points with room for a name, a category and a note on why you were there. The app never types the command for you; that is your keypress.

                Why does a point say “believed to be”?

                /showlocation gives three numbers and no system, and the same numbers mean different places in Stanton and Pyro. So the system on a point is what your logs said about where you were when you copied it — a belief, and the card says what it rests on: an arrival four minutes earlier, or a quantum jump forty minutes earlier. If the logs were wrong or had nothing, set the system on the card; it is yours from then on.

                How far away is a point?

                Copy a fresh /showlocation and the reading lists your nearest points with distances; the Points page measures every card from wherever you last copied. A point in another system is named as not measurable rather than given a number, because the two sets of coordinates are in different frames. The app does not decide how near counts as “there” — a cave mouth and a belt disagree, and you can judge a distance better than it can guess one.

                The overlay will not let me click the game.

                Press its 📌 pin button and clicks pass straight through. To get it back, right-click the tray icon and untick Pin overlay, or press Ctrl+Alt+O. It arrives unpinned so you can place it first.

                How do the Cougar MFD displays work?

                Choose MFD setup… from the tray, place the two display openings on the monitor or monitors behind the frames, then enable and save the layout. The panels use the same local data as the dashboard but keep the answer short: navigation, the next task, cargo, contracts, money and the live feed. They are optional and do not replace the regular overlay.

                Does screenshot reading capture my screen?

                No. The optional reader only reads a screenshot file you saved in Star Citizen's screenshots folder, after you turn it on — every file in that folder, the ones already there included. Untick Watch screenshots to stop, invalidate a reading on the Log tab to have nothing believed from it, or clear them all. It does not capture the display, read game memory or send the image away. The dashboard keeps a dated reading; the overlay and MFDs receive only a small summary.

                Which screens can it read?

                An item tooltip, a ship's Vehicle Loadout Manager (which the Fleet page shows as photographed and the Garage can start a bench from), the mobiGlas map footer, the Contracts and Reputation apps, the Fleet Manager and a commodity kiosk — each checked against what the logs believed and shown with the verdict. The inventory screen cannot be read: its items are icons with no text, so a stash is confirmed one tooltip at a time. A screen it does not know goes to the review queue on the Log tab with its text, which is how each reader so far was written.

                A reading is wrong. What do I do with it?

                On the Log tab each reading has Invalidate, which keeps it in the list — struck through, so the misreading can still be seen — but stops the wallet, the fleet, the fittings and the Now card believing it; and Read again, which puts the same screenshot through the reader as it is now and replaces the old reading. A screen the app learned to read after you photographed it is read at last that way.

                Why does cash on hand say “about”?

                The logs never state a balance. The figure comes from the last screenshot that printed it in a face the reader can read — so far that has been a commodity kiosk; the mobiGlas bar has never read — and the app carries it forward by the transactions logged since. It says “about” because the logs miss some money movement, such as fees, claims or a transaction the parser did not recognise. The next readable screenshot is the check, and says how far off the estimate was.

                Sticks and keybindings

                Where does the Controls page get my bindings from?

                From the game's own keybinding profile, user\client\0\Profiles\default\actionmaps.xml — the file the keybinding screen writes. Settings → Controls reads it against the catalogue of every action the game can bind and shows each stick with its bindings drawn on a picture of it. Nothing is guessed: a control with nothing on it is blank, not empty-looking.

                Is it going to break my keybindings?

                It keeps a copy before it writes, and it keeps one whenever the game writes too — the app looks at the profile every five seconds and files any version it has not seen under the app's own folder, named by time and content. Backups lists them, shows what changed between any two, and restores one. So the state you are in now is already saved before you change anything, and nothing is written into the game folder except the profile itself.

                Why does it refuse to write while the game is running?

                Star Citizen reads the profile at start and rewrites it in play, so anything written underneath a running game is overwritten and lost. With the game open the app offers the other route instead: it writes an import file into user\client\0\controls\mappings\ and tells you the console command — pp_rebindkeys yourname — so the change loads from the keybinding screen without a restart. Close the game and the same change writes straight to the profile.

                My sticks swapped and every binding is on the wrong one. Can that be fixed?

                Yes, and it is the reason the page exists. The game stores a binding against a stick's number, not its name — js2_button11 — and that number is the order Windows enumerated the devices in, which can change when something is unplugged or a USB port is reused. Restoring or exporting a version lets you say which number each stick has become, and every input and options line moves with it, chords included.

                Why is nothing lighting up when I press a button?

                The live reading comes from the tray app, not the dashboard server, because reading a controller needs the Windows side of the app. If the page says so in the note under the sticks, you are on the bare server — start QuantumWake from the tray instead. A stick the profile knows but that is unplugged says that too. One case it deliberately refuses: two of the same product, because the id the game stores names the product, not the device, and lighting the wrong picture would be worse than lighting none.

                Where do the pictures of the sticks come from?

                From Joystick Diagrams, an open-source project with templates for 45 sticks, throttles and panels. They are fetched only if you ask, on the Pictures pane, and kept in the app's folder — nothing is bundled, and no feed is turned on by anything else you switch on. A stick with no template gets a numbered grid built from its real button, axis and switch counts, and you can add your own picture for it.

                Sharing and backups

                What can I send to a friend?

                Settings → Share what you have writes one file with what you tick: your own trades over a window you choose, the blueprints you hold, your jobs, checklists and flight plans, and your points of interest with their notes. Your handle goes on it only if you leave that box ticked. UEX prices are never in it — they are a third party's, not yours to pass on.

                What happens to a file someone sends me?

                Open it from Shared files. It is kept apart from your own work — nothing is merged — so removing it takes it away completely. Its rows are hidden until you choose to show them on that page; then a friend's trades appear on the Cargo page, their blueprints on Jobs → Blueprints, and their points under Shared by others on Points, where Keep as mine copies one across as your own. Anything in the file that does not read — a date in the future, a coordinate that is not a number — is dropped and counted, and the file says how many.

                What is in a backup, and what is not?

                Settings → Back up what you typed: jobs, checklists, flight plans, the mining log, map notes, kits, saved Garage builds, points of interest with their notes, your goal, your wipe line and your label settings — everything re-reading the logs cannot bring back. Sessions, trades and contracts are deliberately not in it: they come back by reading your logs again, and a second copy could disagree with the game. Restoring shows what it would change first, keeps newer work on this machine by default, and never brings back something you deleted on purpose without asking.

                Privacy and support

                Does it touch the game or my account?

                No game memory is read and nothing is injected. Quantum Wake reads Game.log, its backups and, when you ask it to, saved screenshots or copied location text. The game directory is untouched unless you explicitly install item labels or StarStrings.

                Does it phone home or check for updates?

                It sends no identifier, usage or statistics. Version checks and market refreshes are off until you enable them in Settings. Community datasets are also opt-in. Update checks use GitHub's public release feed; nothing is downloaded or installed without your action.

                Something is wrong or missing. Where do I say so?

                Please do. Ask on the Discord, or raise an issue at github.com/peans99/QuantumWake/issues. A report with the source shown beside the number, or the log line itself, is especially useful.
