How the shipped profile is laid out

Every page has its own button, so there is no cycling to learn. Top 1–5: Nav, Task, Act, Cargo, Contract. Right 9 / 10: Money, List. Left 16 / 17 / 18: Status, Feed, Crew. Nav shows where you are, the route and the distance. Task shows the next planned stop and its next unfinished instruction. Act lists the outstanding work at that stop and ticks one off — DONE once to arm, again to confirm. Cargo shows what your plan says to load against what the commodity counters recorded, and never claims to know what is in the hold. Contract, Feed, Crew, Money and List carry the open contract, the live log, who the party channel named, your trading rate and your shopping lists.

Only three buttons remain: 8 is DONE, and 14 / 12 are up and down, which move the cursor on Act and scroll a page too long to fit. All three dim on a page where they have nothing to act on. Previous / next page and Home ship unassigned — with a button for every page they are a second way to do the same thing — and screen brightness and text size are the sliders above rather than four more buttons. All five stay in the list if you want them.

The four rocker switches report as buttons 21–28, and which rocker is which number is not something a datasheet answers — so they ship unassigned. Press one, watch the tester name its number, and bind it above.

USB detection uses Windows joystick slots 1–16 and the default F16 MFD names. If a device is missing, check the Thrustmaster driver and close any T.A.R.G.E.T. profile that replaces the devices with a virtual controller. Duplicate MFD numbers must be changed in the Thrustmaster control panel.

MFD mode is optional. Your regular overlay has its own settings.
